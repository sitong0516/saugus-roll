Fun Facts

Roller skating has inspired inventors, performers, and communities for generations. Here are five facts to take into your next conversation.

Five turns through skating history

  1. Inline skates are older than you might think.

    The Smithsonian's "Volito" inline skate dates to 1823, long before the modern inline-skating trend.

    Read the source

  2. One early design used five wheels.

    The Volito had a single row of five wooden wheels and a curved iron bar at the front that acted as a brake.

    Read the source

  3. The modern quad skate has a 19th-century milestone.

    James Leonard Plimpton invented the quad skate in 1863, a major moment in roller-skating history.

    Read the source

  4. Some exhibition skates were covered in gilt.

    The Smithsonian preserves gilt-covered skates worn by W. M. Drown during roller-skating exhibitions. Their wheels were made of wood.

    Read the source

  5. Rink managers had an entertainment guide in 1886.

    Nicholson's roller skating rink book offered more than sixty attractions with instructions for rink managers. The Library of Congress preserves a copy.

    Read the source
